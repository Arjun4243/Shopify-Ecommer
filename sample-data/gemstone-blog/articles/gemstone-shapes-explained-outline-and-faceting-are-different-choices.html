Gemstone Shapes Explained: Outline and Faceting Are Different Choices

Raza Gems Editorial · 242 words

Round, oval, pear and cushion describe outlines that are easy to recognize. Brilliant and step-cut describe aspects of how facets are arranged. Treating shape and faceting as separate parts of your brief makes it easier to explain a design and compare candidates without relying on a single broad label.

Start with the silhouette Choose the outline that best suits your project. A long silhouette and a compact square create different visual starting points, even before you compare the material. Draw a few simple outlines on paper and note which proportions feel right for the intended piece.

Look inside the outline Faceting arrangements influence the pattern of reflections you see. When reviewing candidates, ask for images that show the whole stone and request a short video. Write down the visual effect you enjoy rather than assuming every stone sharing one outline will look the same.

Read the dimensions Record length, width and depth, not just the shape name. An oval description does not tell the jeweller exactly how elongated the item is. If an existing setting is involved, have the maker assess fit using the selected stone's actual measurements.

Create a usable brief Your final note can combine material, outline, preferred proportions, visual references and intended use. Ask the cutter or jeweller to explain any practical trade-offs for the actual stone. This turns a list of attractive shapes into a design discussion with clear priorities and room for informed advice.
