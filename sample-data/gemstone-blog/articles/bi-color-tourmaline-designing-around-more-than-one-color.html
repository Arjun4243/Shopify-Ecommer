Bi-Color Tourmaline: Designing Around More Than One Color

Raza Gems Editorial · 253 words

A gemstone with more than one visible color asks for a slightly different design conversation. Instead of choosing a single shade, you are choosing an arrangement: where each color appears and how the outline presents the combination. Begin with that arrangement when exploring bi-color tourmaline.

Understand the visual feature Tourmaline can show different color zones within the same crystal. Some cut stones display a visible combination of colors, including pink and green. The exact arrangement belongs to the individual stone, so a representative photograph cannot show you how every bi-color example will look.

Request the exact item Ask for photographs of the stone being offered, including its complete outline. Mark which end or side you would like at the top of the finished design. This gives the jeweller a specific orientation to discuss instead of leaving a major visual choice unspoken.

Compare composition rather than labels Place shortlisted images side by side and note the distribution you prefer. Record the measurements and item numbers. If two stones both use the same trade description, keep comparing their actual appearance; matching words do not make them interchangeable for your project.

Agree on the design direction Bring the stone's photographs and measurements to the maker. Discuss how the setting will frame the colors and whether your preferred orientation has practical constraints. Confirm identity, treatment disclosure and the selected item in writing. A well-planned bi-color design begins with the individual stone's composition and carries that idea through to the finished piece.
