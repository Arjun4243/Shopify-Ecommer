Sapphire Beyond Blue: A Guide to Choosing Your Color

Raza Gems Editorial · 256 words

Blue may be the first color you associate with sapphire, but it is only the beginning of the conversation. Before comparing individual stones, decide whether you want a familiar blue, a softer pink or another color direction. Keeping that preference visible will help you ask better questions and avoid collecting unrelated options.

Know the gem family Sapphire belongs to corundum. The name is used for corundum in colors other than the red associated with ruby. A listing's color name describes only part of the item; it is not a substitute for identifying the stone or disclosing its treatment history.

Compare a small selection Ask for a few candidates in your chosen color family rather than every available sapphire. Request consistent photographs and note which appearance you prefer before studying the descriptions. Write down whether you want a bold centerpiece, a quieter everyday design or a pair for earrings.

Prepare a setting brief Give your jeweller the actual length, width and depth of each shortlisted stone. Include the preferred metal, the overall style and any existing piece it should complement. If you are planning a pair, ask to compare both stones together rather than relying on separate photographs.

Keep the evidence with the item Save the seller's item reference, treatment statement, report details and quotation in one place. Ask for clarification when any of those records refer to a different stone. Your goal is a clear connection between the sapphire you selected, its description and the information supplied with it—not simply a beautiful photograph.
