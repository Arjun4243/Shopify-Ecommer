How to Read a Colored Gemstone Laboratory Report

Raza Gems Editorial · 255 words

A laboratory report is most useful when you know which questions it addresses. Begin with the specific stone you are considering and the information you want confirmed. Then read the document as a record of findings, keeping its scope separate from the seller's marketing description.

Check the service described GIA's colored stone services can address identity, natural or laboratory-grown status and detectable treatments. Some report services also offer geographic origin opinions for eligible materials. Read the report type and actual results rather than assuming every document includes the same investigations.

Match the report to the item Compare the identifying information supplied with the report against the seller's listing. Review measurements, weight where applicable, description and reference details. Ask the seller to resolve inconsistencies and explain how the report is connected to the item you intend to receive.

Preserve the exact wording Record the laboratory's name, report number and the findings relevant to your decision. If the laboratory offers an official verification service, use that official route. A seller's uploaded image or a logo on a page is not a replacement for reading and checking the actual document.

Keep other decisions separate Use the report alongside your visual comparison, design requirements and the written commercial terms. Ask separately for any appraisal or price opinion you require; do not assume it is included in the report you have been shown. The best reading habit is simple: establish what the document covers, connect it to the stone and keep unanswered questions visible.
